Coming to Singapore for knee treatment

Dr Lai Kah Weng, Orthopaedic Surgeon · 16 September 2026

Travelling for treatment adds a layer of planning that a local appointment does not need. Most of it is administrative, and most of it is easier done before you fly than after you land.

This page covers that planning. It describes how appointments, records and billing work at this clinic. It does not say anything about your knee — what treatment suits you depends on findings only an examination can establish.

Bring your records, not just your reports

The single thing that most often wastes a first consultation is arriving without the imaging itself.

A radiologist’s written report is a summary of what one person saw. The images are the evidence, and a surgeon will want to look at them directly, because what matters for a treatment decision is often not what the report chose to emphasise. Bring the disc, the film, or the access link your imaging centre gave you — and bring the report as well, since it tells you when and where the scan was done.

If you have had more than one scan over time, bring all of them. A comparison between two dates frequently says more than either one alone.

Worth bringing:

  • Imaging of the knee — X-rays, MRI, ultrasound or CT, with the reports.
  • A referral letter, if a doctor at home has written one. You do not need a referral to be seen privately here, but if someone has already examined the knee, their findings save repeating work.
  • Notes of any previous surgery on that knee, including the operation record if you can obtain it.
  • A list of your regular medications, including anything for blood thinning.
  • Your passport, and your insurance policy details if you have cover.

If you have been keeping your own note of what the knee does — when it gives way, when it swells, what you have stopped being able to do — bring that too.

Arranging the appointment

Appointments here are requested rather than booked directly, so that a staff member can confirm a time with you rather than leaving you to guess at one. You send your contact details and the days that suit you, and the clinic comes back to you by whichever method you chose.

For anyone travelling, do that well before you book flights. Confirm the appointment first, then the travel — not the other way round.

The appointment request form asks for no medical details at all. If you would rather speak to someone, the clinic number is on every page of this site and staff answer it during opening hours, Singapore time.

What it costs, and when you find out

Consultation fees are published on the fees page.

Fees for a procedure are not, because they depend on what is done and where it is done. If a procedure is being considered after your consultation, the clinic prepares a written estimate that sets out the surgeon’s fee, the anaesthetist’s fee and the facility charge as separate items, with implants and consumables where they apply. You get that in writing before anything is scheduled.

An estimate is not a final bill. The amount can change if what is found during a procedure changes what is done, or if your length of stay changes.

Insurance

Bring your policy documents rather than a policy number. Overseas policies vary widely in what they will pay for treatment abroad, whether they require pre-authorisation, and whether they pay the hospital directly or reimburse you afterwards — and the answer determines what you need to arrange before you travel, not after.

Clinic staff handle claims and pre-authorisation where a policy allows it. Ask them early; some insurers need several working days.

After you go home

Plan for follow-up before you leave, not once you have landed.

Whether that is a review here, a handover to a doctor at home, or a combination, it is a question to settle during the consultation, while everyone involved is in the same room. Ask what records you will be given to take with you, who should see them, and what would count as a reason to seek help sooner.

What this page cannot tell you

The question travelling patients most often want answered in advance is how soon after a procedure they can fly home.

That is a clinical question, not an administrative one. It depends on what was done, on your own circumstances, and on findings that do not exist until you have been examined — so nobody can answer it honestly from a website, and you should be wary of any site that tries. Ask it at the consultation, before anything is scheduled, and plan your travel around the answer you get.

For anything urgent while you are in Singapore, do not wait for an appointment. Call 995 or go to the nearest emergency department.

This article is general information, not advice about your own knee. For emergencies, call 995 or go to the nearest emergency department.
